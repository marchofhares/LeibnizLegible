# Open ground-truth datasets for German Kurrent, c. 1600–1750: inventory as of 25–26 October 2026

*Conventions. I retrieved everything on 2026-10-25/26 from primary records: the HTR-United catalogue YAML on GitHub, Zenodo record pages and REST API, Hugging Face dataset and model cards, raw GitHub READMEs, and Transkribus model pages. "Verified" means I opened the record or card itself; where I only saw a search snippet, or a fetch failed, the entry says so. "(older)" marks something published before 2024 that is still downloadable. Sizes are the numbers printed by the source; the few figures I counted myself are labelled "my count". My own reasoning appears only under "Inferences". Items the project already uses (GWLB letter transcriptions 1708–1716, Bullinger HTR, Beckert's Dresden sets, Kurrent Trace, PHILIUMM, §70 Academy volumes) appear only where I found something new about them.*

## 1. What does the HTR-United catalogue list for German-language handwriting, 1550–1800?

### Takeaway
The catalogue's data file (138 entries on 2026-10-25) holds six German-language manuscript entries that overlap 1550–1800: three Dresden court-diary sets (1653–1673), Basel Urfehdenbuch X (1563–69), Königsfelden (catalogue dates 1292–1570) and the Bullinger HTR set (1523–75). None of its German entries is later than 1673 (the latest Dresden diary), so it offers nothing for 1680–1750, and several newer relevant sets (Leopold I letters, FoNDUE-DE-MSS-18, the Hanse sets) are not in it.

### Cited Findings
- The catalogue data file is `htr-united.yml` on the `master` branch of HTR-United/htr-united; it parsed to 138 entries on 2026-10-25 ([htr-united.yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HTR-United/htr-united/master/htr-united.yml)). The web view at [htr-united.github.io/catalog.html](https://htr-united.github.io/catalog.html) is script-rendered and returned no entries to my fetcher, so I read the YAML.
- German-language manuscript entries overlapping 1550–1800, values as given in the YAML ([htr-united.yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HTR-United/htr-united/master/htr-united.yml)):
  - Dresdner Hofdiarium 1665 (Mscr.Dresd.K.80): licence CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0; 10 pages; Alto-XML; eScriptorium + Kraken; language deu, script Latn qualified "Kurrent"; 1665.
  - Dresdner Hofdiarium 1673 (Mscr.Dresd.K.117): CC-BY 4.0; 20 pages; Alto-XML.
  - Dresdner Hofdiarium 1653–56 (Mscr.Dresd.K.113): CC-BY 4.0; 12 pages; Alto-XML.
  - StABS Ratsbücher O10, Urfehdenbuch X (Basel): CC-BY-SA 4.0 in the catalogue; 8000 lines; Page-XML; Transkribus; 1563–1569. The Zenodo record itself says CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([Zenodo 5153263](https://zenodo.org/records/5153263)).
  - Charters and Records of Königsfelden Abbey and Bailiwick (title 1308–1662): CC-BY 4.0; 60000 lines; Page-XML; Transkribus; languages lat + deu; the `time` field says 1292–1570.
  - Bullinger HTR Dataset: CC-BY-SA 4.0; 165,673 lines; Image-Text-Pairs; 1523–1575; lat + deu (already used by the project).
- German-language entries in the catalogue that fall outside the period: Incunabula Reichenau (prints, 1470–1510, CC-BY-SA 4.0, 2200 pages); Klosterneuburg Cod. 48 (1440–1449, CC-BY 4.0, 68 pages / 4605 lines); Inzigkofen manuscripts (15th c., CC-BY 4.0, 9726 lines); Early New High German diplomatic set v2 (1480–1485, CC-BY 4.0, 2177 lines / 110,618 characters); NZZ black-letter prints (1780–1946, CC-BY 4.0, 43,173 lines); OCR-D gt_structure_text (prints, 1500–1900, CC-BY-SA 4.0); FONDUE-MLT-ART (prints, 1842–1961); TranscriboQuest medieval sets ([htr-united.yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HTR-United/htr-united/master/htr-united.yml)).
- A text search of the YAML for "leopold", "early-modern-german", "hanse" and "fondue" found no German 17th/18th-century Kurrent entry beyond those above; the FoNDUE entries it lists are French, English, Spanish, Italian, art-catalogue or multilingual sets ([htr-united.yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HTR-United/htr-united/master/htr-united.yml)). Two relevant repositories nevertheless ship their own `htr-united.yml` and so are catalogue-ready: the Leopold I deposit ([file in Zenodo 17911243](https://zenodo.org/records/17911243/files/htr-united.yml)) and FoNDUE-DE-MSS-18 ([raw yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FoNDUE-HTR/FONDUE-DE-MSS-18/main/htr-united.yml)).
- The HTR-United GitHub organisation lists 30 public repositories (CREMMA, lectaurep, timeus, tooling and so on); none is a German Kurrent dataset ([GitHub org](https://github.com/HTR-United), from a repository search listing).

### Inferences
- The catalogue is a poor discovery channel for German Early Modern handwriting; the useful sets sit on Zenodo, Hugging Face and project GitHub repositories (sections 2 and 3).
- The catalogue and the Zenodo records disagree on licence for the Basel set (SA versus NC-SA) and, across the three Dresden sets, among themselves (see section 5); the catalogue's licence column should not be trusted without checking the deposit.

### Gaps
- I could not render the live catalogue page, so entries merged after the YAML snapshot I read would be missed.
- The `time` fields are as authors typed them (Königsfelden: title 1308–1662, YAML 1292–1570), so period filtering on the catalogue alone is unreliable.

## 2. Which Zenodo, Hugging Face and GitHub datasets exist for 17th/18th-century German Kurrent (including Austrian and Swiss material, 2024–2026 releases)?

### Takeaway
Verified open, in-period (1600–1750) page-level ground truth is small: the Dresden diaries (42 pages), the Leopold I autograph letters (271 pages, new December 2025) and the Hanse recesses on Hugging Face (1,298 17th-century page rows, new July 2026); READ Bozen, Königsfelden and the Basel Urfehdenbuch are older and have uncertain or partial overlap with the period. Two further sources (Zürich Stillstandsprotokolle TEI and Tübingen's Konsilien 1659–1665 with IIIF and Transkribus text) are text-only or edition-style and need alignment.

#### 2B. Details and caveats per dataset
- **Leopold I letters (new, December 2025).** The README describes digitised autograph letters of Emperor Leopold I to his Spanish ambassadors Lamberg (1655–1658) and Pötting (1662–1667); "Writing: German kurrent and Latin cursive"; "Leopold's script is difficult to read"; three folders (HHStA Große Korrespondenz K. 33.1 Buch 1662–1665, Buch 1666–1667; OÖLA Herrschaftsarchiv Steyr Schachtel 1232 Fasz. 21); one PageXML file and one JPEG per page ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HTR-School-Vienna/2025--early-modern-german/main/README.md)). Its `htr-united.yml` gives hands "count 1, precision exact", script Latf qualified "Kurrentschrift", production software Transkribus, not automatically aligned, and cites the print edition of the Pötting letters (Pribram and Landwehr, 1903) ([htr-united.yml](https://zenodo.org/records/17911243/files/htr-united.yml)). Transcription conventions: abbreviations "quietly resolved" with a few kept, punctuation modernised, later archival marks not transcribed, tags for deletions, additions, ciphers and unclear text ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HTR-School-Vienna/2025--early-modern-german/main/README.md)). Licence wording: "All transcriptions are licensed under the Creative Commons 4 licence. The license of the images … remains with the Haus-, Hof- und Staatsarchiv and the Oberösterreichisches Landesarchiv" ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HTR-School-Vienna/2025--early-modern-german/main/README.md)); the Zenodo record's `LICENSE.MD` begins "Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International" ([LICENSE.MD](https://zenodo.org/records/17911243/files/LICENSE.MD)); the Zenodo metadata says CC BY 4.0 ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/17911243)). The archive zips are 382.6 MB, 52.9 MB and 39.3 MB ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/17911243)).
- **Dresden court diaries.** All three Zenodo records describe "a typical late 17th century Saxon kurrent ('Kanzleikurrent'), with occasional words written in bastarda or fraktur-like script", scans from SLUB under the Public Domain Mark, and transcription guidelines adapted from DTABf-M (u/v kept as written, long s distinguished, abbreviation signs kept as letters, ß for the sz ligature in Kurrent) ([K.113](https://zenodo.org/records/15303398); [K.117](https://zenodo.org/records/15303243); [K.80](https://zenodo.org/records/14356190)). Each description also states "The dataset can be used under the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 License" while the record metadata says CC BY 4.0 (same three records). A third party's Hugging Face mirror of the K.80 sample labels the transcription `cc-by-nc-sa-4.0` and the images Public Domain Mark ([HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Ma-Ri-Ba-Ku/Cinoc-Dresdner.Hofdiarium.1665-Mscr.Dresd.K.80)). Of the three, only K.113 appears among the sources the Party base model was derived from ([Party record](https://zenodo.org/records/20642057)).
- **FGHO Hanse recesses (Hugging Face, 2026).** `hanse-kurrent-xvii-rawxml` holds "minutes of the low German town assemblies (Niederdeutsche Städtetage) from the 17th century from various archives", transcribed under the "Hanse.Quellen.Lesen!" guidelines (CC BY 4.0, Zenodo 20746642) ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fgho/hanse-kurrent-xvii-rawxml); [guidelines](https://zenodo.org/records/20746642)). Siblings: 16th c. lines `hanse-kurrent-xvi-lines` with 27,217 lines (25,856 train, 1,361 test; about 7.9 GB), text in "middle low German and New High German" ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fgho/hanse-kurrent-xvi-lines)); 16th c. pages `hanse-kurrent-xvi-rawxml` with 1,145 rows, 13.6 GB ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fgho/hanse-kurrent-xvi-rawxml)); 15th c. `hanse-kurrent-xv` with 429 rows ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fgho/hanse-kurrent-xv)). The text-only `hanse-niederdeutsch-plaintext` (14th–15th c. Middle Low German) is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0 because part derives from the non-commercial ReN corpus ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fgho/hanse-niederdeutsch-plaintext)). The project behind it started in 2020 as a citizen-science effort of FGHO and the Lübeck city archive ([Transkribus blog](https://blog.transkribus.org/en/hanse.quelle.lesen-citizen-science-hansemuseum-fgho), from a search snippet). The matching public Transkribus model "Hanserezesse 17th Century v.5" (ID 222285, 2024-11-18) was trained on 947 pages, 23,096 lines, 129,000 words, validation CER 5.08%, and its page does not say whether the training data is public ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/hanserezesse-17th-century-v5)).
- **READ Bozen / ICFHR 2016 (older).** "A subset of documents from the Ratsprotokolle collection composed of minutes of the council meetings held from 1470 to 1805 (about 30.000 pages) … written in Early Modern German. The number of writers is unknown … The training dataset is composed of 400 pages", PAGE format at line level ([Zenodo 218236](https://zenodo.org/records/218236)); v1.2.0 adds the competition test set ([Zenodo 1297399](https://zenodo.org/records/1297399)). A later paper reports a line split of 8,349 training, 1,040 validation and 1,138 test lines for READ 2016, noting its split differs slightly from the official one ([arXiv 2012.03868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03868), from a search snippet). The records do not say how many pages fall in 1600–1750.
- **READ Konzilsprotokolle (older).** "8770 trainscribed textlines of handwritten historical documents from the late 18th century", images from Dirk Alvermann (Universitätsarchiv Greifswald), train/test lists provided ([Zenodo 215383](https://zenodo.org/records/215383)). The ICFHR 2018 READ competition set (heterogeneous German documents, 1.37 GB general data, CC BY 4.0) does not state its periods ([Zenodo 1442182](https://zenodo.org/records/1442182)).
- **Königsfelden (older).** Edition "Die Urkunden und Akten des Klosters und des Oberamts Königsfelden 1308–1662" is published at koenigsfelden.uzh.ch; the Zenodo deposit holds charters and records digitised with the Aargau State Archives ([Zenodo 5179361](https://zenodo.org/records/5179361)); the edition data (19.1 MB, version 2024-04-09, CC BY 4.0) is separate ([Zenodo 10950031](https://zenodo.org/records/10950031)). The share of material after 1550 is not stated; a Hugging Face re-upload tagged "post-1500" gives its period as 1291–1550 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dh-unibe/image-text_koenigsfelden-charters-post-1500)).
- **StABS Urfehdenbuch X (older).** Images and text aligned with Transkribus text-to-image; ALTO and PAGE XML; derived from the TEI of "Die Urfehdebücher der Stadt Basel – digitale Edition"; "CC-BY-NC-SA: The license is inherited from the project" ([Zenodo 5153263](https://zenodo.org/records/5153263)). The record's creators are Hodel, Schoch and Dängeli (same record).
- **FoNDUE-DE-MSS-18.** Content table: Luzern, Luzerner Bibliothek, first half of the 18th century, transcription by Ch. Riedo and S. Gabay; "Annotation is CC-BY. Images belong to the digital libraries" ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FoNDUE-HTR/FONDUE-DE-MSS-18/main/README.md)); metadata: hands count unknown, eScriptorium + Kraken, SegmOnto guidelines ([htr-united.yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FoNDUE-HTR/FONDUE-DE-MSS-18/main/htr-united.yml)). A sibling "FONDUE-DE-MSS-16-PR" is cited as a source of the Party model but its README returned 404 to me, so I could not verify it ([Party record](https://zenodo.org/records/20642057)).
- **mkn-kurrent-gt.** Page-XML, language deu, 1765–1806, transcription guidelines OCR-D GT Level 2, project Gemein-Nachrichten der Herrnhuter Brüdergemeine, 19,606 text lines on 771 pages ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bertsky/mkn-kurrent-gt/main/README.md)).
- **Tübingen Konsilien 1659–1665 (UAT 84/13), OpenDigi.** The object page carries a IIIF Presentation 2 manifest with 553 canvases (my count from the manifest), terms "free of known copyrights … (public domain)", and a "TEI: Complete project" link that opens the full transcription ("Transcribed with Transkribus") ([OpenDigi](https://opendigi.ub.uni-tuebingen.de/opendigi/UAT_084_13); [manifest](https://opendigi.ub.uni-tuebingen.de/opendigi/UAT_084_13/manifest); [text view](https://opendigi.ub.uni-tuebingen.de/opendigi/UAT_084_13/ocr); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.20345/digitue.23865)). The sample text is German with Latin legal phrases mixed in ([text view](https://opendigi.ub.uni-tuebingen.de/opendigi/UAT_084_13/ocr)). The library describes its juristic consilia series as 269 volumes, mostly German and Latin, in cursive hands ([2021 slides](https://ocr-bw.bib.uni-mannheim.de/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/2021-06-09_Vortrag_Juristische_Konsilien.pdf), from a search snippet), covering 1602–1879 for the digitised selection ([uni-tuebingen.de](https://uni-tuebingen.de/de/179298), from a search snippet). UB Mannheim used "Konsilien 1659–1665. Tübingen" as ground truth in its first German-handwriting training run ([Mannheim wiki](https://github.com/UB-Mannheim/kraken/wiki/Training-German-Handwriting)). Whether the transcription is hand-corrected is not stated on the object page.

- Those that touch the early modern period: `image-text_aaeb-xiv-xvii` (Archives de l'ancien Evêché de Bâle; 2,566 rows; card says Early Modern German, period "1400–1500") with a line variant of 62,417 lines / 2,288,208 characters from 2,068 pages ([page card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dh-unibe/image-text_aaeb-xiv-xvii); [lines card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dh-unibe/image-text_aaeb-xiv-xvii-lines)); `-part-2` with 121 rows in Latin, French and German ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dh-unibe/image-text_aaeb-xiv-xvii-part-2)); Zürich `rats-und-richtebuecher_xv-xvi` (9,885 rows; 1400–1550; line variant 139,708 lines, 4,822,137 characters) ([lines card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dh-unibe/image-text_rats-und-richtebuecher_xv-xvi-lines)); `data-towerbooks-textlines` (47,845 lines; no description or licence on the card) ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dh-unibe/data-towerbooks-textlines)); `thodel/ratsmanuale-lines` (2,654 lines) and `thodel/missiven-lines` (2,480 lines), both "unknown_project" ([ratsmanuale](https://huggingface.co/datasets/thodel/ratsmanuale-lines); [missiven](https://huggingface.co/datasets/thodel/missiven-lines)); `image-text_bullinger-autoren` (8,022 rows of letters, 1530–1600, Latin and Early Modern German, "various archives", tagged MIT; the card does not say how it relates to the CC BY-SA Bullinger HTR dataset) ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dh-unibe/image-text_bullinger-autoren)).

#### 2F. Checked and excluded

#### 2G. Open models trained on this material (licence-relevant)

### Inferences
- Counting only verified in-period page-level sets, open material is about 1,611 pages (42 Dresden + 271 Leopold + 1,298 Hanse 17th-century rows), and 1,298 of those sit in a Hugging Face set whose rights statement is a bare "MIT" tag.
- In-period private-letter material is limited to one hand (Leopold I); everything else is chancery, council, court or diary writing.
- Swiss coverage is strongest for council, court and monastery records (Basel, Königsfelden, Zürich); Austrian-Habsburg coverage is the Leopold I letters plus the Bozen (South Tyrol) council minutes; I found no open ground truth for university matriculation registers or Pietist letter collections.
- The Hanse HF sets are probably the training data behind the public Transkribus Hanserezesse models and the fgho TrOCR models; the cards imply this but do not state it.

### Gaps
- No open ground truth found for university matriculations (the Rostock Matrikelportal pairs transcriptions with register images but I found no licence; section 3), Pietist correspondence (the Francke portal is non-commercial only and I found no Francke HTR project), or private-letter hands of 1680–1750 other than Leopold I.
- Page counts within 1600–1750 for READ Bozen, Königsfelden and the dh-unibe sets are not stated by their records.
- Image availability for mkn-kurrent-gt and the exact contents of the Königsfelden Zenodo files were not itemised; I did not download any archive.
- FONDUE-DE-MSS-16-PR (cited by Party) could not be retrieved; the FoNDUE-HTR organisation page showed only 10 of 25 repositories and no DE entries ([org page](https://github.com/FoNDUE-HTR)).
- GitHub-wide discovery was limited in this session to keyword search that returned mostly unrelated "Kurrent" software; unlisted German GT repositories may exist.

## 3. Which digital editions publish transcriptions of 17th/18th-century German letters or drafts together with page images or IIIF links (retro-alignment candidates)?

### Takeaway
The best-documented candidates are the Dresden diary TEI texts (SLUB scans under the Public Domain Mark), the Tübingen Konsilien 1659–1665 (IIIF plus Transkribus text, public domain) and the Zürich Stillstandsprotokolle TEI (CC BY-SA 4.0, images external). The Heidelberg Liselotte letters and the Herzog August Bibliothek's Christian II diaries are real digital editions but their reuse terms are share-alike or unverified; the Francke portal is non-commercial only, and the Spener, Gottsched and "Fürstinnenkorrespondenz" material I found is print or has no confirmed online data.

### Cited Findings
- **Dresden Hofdiarium TEI (Stefan Beckert).** The 1665 diary (Mscr.Dresd.K.80) is a "vollständige händische Transkription" in TEI-XML plus a PDF reading version; the description says entries are separate `<div>` elements with dates, persons and places tagged, that version 1 lacked authority data and used `<ex>` rather than `<choice>` for abbreviations, and the current file is "Release 2" (1.1 MB, CC BY 4.0) ([Zenodo 14932508](https://zenodo.org/records/14932508)). The 1653–56 and 1673 texts came from a semi-automatic workflow: line segmentation in Transkribus, a model fine-tuned in eScriptorium on 15 training pages (base model german_handwriting_20230512), HTR errors corrected by an LLM (Claude 3.7 Sonnet), TEI markup by the same LLM, then checked by hand against the scans; the authors state the result is "nicht immer zeichengenau, sondern sinnerhaltend" ([Zenodo 15228309](https://zenodo.org/records/15228309); [Zenodo 15295553](https://zenodo.org/records/15295553)). The records point to reading versions at the Deutsches Textarchiv (dresden_hofdiarium_1657 for 1653–56, dresden_hofdiarium_1674 for 1673) (same records). SLUB's scans are under the Public Domain Mark ([Zenodo 15303398](https://zenodo.org/records/15303398)).
- **Tübingen Konsilien 1659–1665** (IIIF manifest, text view, public-domain terms): see section 2B ([OpenDigi](https://opendigi.ub.uni-tuebingen.de/opendigi/UAT_084_13)).
- **Zürich Stillstandsprotokolle des 17. Jahrhunderts (Staatsarchiv Zürich, project eStPZH).** TEI-XML conversions of protocols first transcribed serially in Word; 35 parishes; the earliest protocols start 1631 (Brütten) and 1634 (Laufen), broadly from 1636/37; the protocols are written by the parish pastor, "often fleeting, hard-to-read notes in the individual hand of the pastor"; the description warns of possible conversion errors, especially for square brackets used for editorial comments and page breaks; licence CC BY-SA 4.0, version 2.0, modified 2025-07-14, zip 3.5 MB ([Zenodo 3985144](https://zenodo.org/records/3985144)). The deposit (a 3.5 MB zip plus XML files) is text only; the record points to the archive's online portal.
- **Königsfelden edition data** (19.1 MB, CC BY 4.0, version 2024-04-09), supplement to the GitHub repository Staatsarchiv-Aargau/koenigsfelden-data ([Zenodo 10950031](https://zenodo.org/records/10950031)).
- **Heidelberg: "Elisabeth Charlotte (Liselotte) von der Pfalz – digital. Die Heidelberger Briefe"** (DOI 10.11588/edition.lld, 2023). The edition covers "the 53 letters preserved in the Heidelberg University Library"; it states she wrote about 60,000 letters, about half in German, of which about 6,000 survive; transcription and editing are in TEI-XML (heiEDITIONS); facsimiles are shown through DWork ([edition page](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/en/lld/index.html)). Terms: edited text CC BY-SA 4.0; facsimiles are "in the public domain" without a formal mark; the page does not mention TEI download or IIIF; the years covered are not stated ([terms of use](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/en/lld/informationen/nutzungsbedingungen.html)). Search results list items addressed to Madame de Maintenon and Madame de Ludres ([Heid. Hs. 3834,3](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/heidhs3834_3)), so the 53 may include French; the language split is not stated. A 2026 chapter reportedly describes a 2024–2025 pilot for a complete edition and about 5,800 surviving letters in several archives including Hannover ([chapter, DOI 10.17885/heiup.1634.c23606](https://heiup.uni-heidelberg.de/catalog/view/1634/2789/132787), snippet only: the page was blocked to my fetcher).
- **Herzog August Bibliothek and Freiburg: diaries of Prince Christian II of Anhalt-Bernburg (1599–1656).** HAB's project page describes a digital edition with XML-coded transcription, colour scans of the source, summaries, translations, registers and a glossary: about 17,400 pages in 23 volumes, "mostly in the prince's own hand", DFG-funded, listed duration November 2013 to October 2025 (completion not stated), edition at diglib.hab.de/edoc/ed000228/start.htm, portal www.tagebuch-christian-ii-anhalt.de, PURL diglib.hab.de/?link=043; the page gives no licence ([HAB project page](https://www.hab.de/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/17000)). A HAB press release of 2015-03-25 says the diary covers 1621–1656, the manuscript is in the Landeshauptarchiv Sachsen-Anhalt (Dessau-Roßlau), the diarist switches between German and "above all French, Italian and Latin", the pilot phase covers about 1,500 pages (January 1635 to August 1637), a facsimile can be viewed beside the edition text, and the edition is offered online "under the principle of Open Access" without naming a licence ([idw press release](https://idw-online.de/en/news628178)). A 2023 RIDE review praises the implementation but criticises missing technical documentation of workflow and data archiving ([RIDE via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/9aa745b5db6242a782c355275fb8dbe6), snippet only). The edition, the portal and the PURL all returned HTTP 503 to my fetcher, so I could not check data format, IIIF or the terms of use.
- **Basel Urfehdebücher digital edition** (GAMS Graz, 2016): the source of the Basel ground truth; its CC BY-NC-SA licence is inherited by the Zenodo deposit ([Zenodo 5153263](https://zenodo.org/records/5153263)).
- **Francke Foundations, Halle.** The portal's "Transcribed Letters" collection shows transcriptions of selected letters of August Hermann Francke next to the digital versions in a synoptic format; the page gives no letter count, date range, download option or licence ([portal](https://digital.francke-halle.de/fstb?lang=en)). The terms of use printed in the portal's PDFs read: "All digital documents of the Francke-Portal are protected by copyright. They may be downloaded and printed only for non-commercial educational, research and private purposes"; commercial or institutional use or publication needs prior written permission from the Study Center, which may charge a fee ([terms as printed in a portal PDF](https://digital.francke-halle.de/download/pdf/252404.pdf)).
- **Spener letters (SAW Leipzig).** Published as printed Mohr Siebeck volumes (Frankfurt period 1666–1686, Dresden period 1686–1691, plus the Francke and Rechenberg correspondences); I found no open digital or TEI version ([SAW project page](https://www.saw-leipzig.de/en/research/projects/edition-der-briefe-philipp-jakob-speners-1635-1705-vor-allem-aus-der-berliner-zeit-1691-1705); [Mohr Siebeck](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/briefe-aus-der-dresdener-zeit-1686-1691-9783161654503)).
- **Gottsched correspondence (SAW Leipzig).** A historical-critical print edition based on about 4,700 letters from Gottsched's estate in the Leipzig university library, 31 volumes planned according to the project page; several volumes are free PDFs on OAPEN, licence not confirmed in my results; no TEI or image data found ([SAW project page](https://www.saw-leipzig.de/en/research/projects/johann-christoph-and-luise-adelgunde-victorie-gottsched-correspondence-a-correspondence-network-in-the-age-of-enlightenment-historical-critical-edition); [OAPEN](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/52243)).
- **"Fürstinnenkorrespondenz."** I found no digital edition under this name. The nearest match is the DFG project "Frühneuzeitliche Fürstinnenkorrespondenzen im mitteldeutschen Raum" (Jena, corpus compiled 2010–2013): 564 autograph and 36 non-autograph letters of 15 central German princesses, 1546–1756, most from 1546–1615; I found no statement that the corpus is online ([review PDF](https://db-thueringen.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/dbt_derivate_00044675/Luehr_Genderspezifik.pdf); [EADH project entry](https://eadh.org/node/771)).
- **Leibniz Academy edition and Leibniz-Archiv Hannover** (new details on a source the project already uses). The Series III page lists checked transcriptions of the correspondence for 1708–1716 and unchecked ones for 1710–1714 in the GWLB repository, plus Series III volumes 5–9 online and a Vorausedition of III,10 as PDF without long-term archiving ([Series III](https://www.leibnizedition.de/en/series/series-iii/)). The unchecked 1714 file (version 1, October 2020) is CC BY-NC 4.0 at the Göttingen Academy repository ([rep.adw-goe.de](https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2473/LAA-Transkriptionen1714nue-v1.pdf), snippet only).
- **Deutsches Textarchiv.** The DTA's manuscript extension DTABf-M is the schema behind Beckert's guidelines ([DTABf-M transcription guidelines](https://kaskade.dwds.de/~haaf/dtabf_en/transkription.html), from a search result); I found no catalogue of 1600–1750 letter manuscripts in the DTA beyond the Dresden reading versions.
- **University matriculation registers: Rostock Matrikelportal.** It covers student registers from 1419; each entry is transcribed and linked to a digitised image of the (partly handwritten) original entry; an English Wikipedia entry gives about 186,000 datasets. I found no licence for the portal's transcriptions or images; the University Library Rostock's digital images are offered under CC BY-NC-SA on another site whose terms say they apply only to contents visualised there ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock_Matrikelportal); [University of Rostock page](https://www.zm.uni-rostock.de/en/digitisation-archiving/university-archives-and-custody/rostock-matriculation-portal/); [UBR image terms](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/en/iwd/informationen/nutzungsbedingungen/ubr/lizenz.html); all snippet-level). Hofmeister's printed edition covers 1611–1694 and 1694–1789 (same snippets).
- **Kepler and Hevelius.** For Kepler I found scanned print: the Bavarian Academy's publication server hosts open-access PDFs of Gesammelte Werke volumes (for example the letters of 1590–1599), and TIB Hannover holds "Johannes Kepler in seinen Briefen" (German, 1930) under Public Domain Mark 1.0 ([BAdW server](https://publikationen.badw.de/en/003396520); [TIB](https://goobi.tib.eu/viewer/!metadata/876592167/24/-); both snippet-level). For Hevelius the results show a critical print edition (Brepols; more than 2,000 letters and about 430 correspondents; a volume's languages listed as English, Latin, French) and the subscription database Electronic Enlightenment, with no mention of German material ([Oxford CofK](https://cofk.history.ox.ac.uk/tag/johannes-hevelius/index.html); [IAHS](https://aihs-iahs.org/en/node/1136); snippet-level).
- **Acta Pacis Westphalicae digital** (University of Bonn and Bavarian State Library, online since 2014): the historical-critical edition of the Westphalian peace congress sources (instructions, correspondence, protocols and diaries); Bonn states 40 of 48 volumes are freely available online; the results mention no manuscript images ([APW Digital](https://digitale-sammlungen.de/en/acta-pacis-westphalicae-apw-digital); [Bonn](https://www.igw.uni-bonn.de/fnzrlg/en/digital-ressources/apw)). One search for "Briefe und Akten zur Geschichte des Dreißigjährigen Krieges" found nothing.

### Inferences
- By effort, the strongest retro-alignment targets are (1) the Tübingen Konsilien (text and IIIF in one place, public domain, but correction status unknown), (2) the Dresden 1665 manual TEI (clean text; scans under PDM), (3) the Zürich Stillstandsprotokolle (clean licence-wise apart from SA, pastors' individual hands, but images must be fetched from the archive and page markers are error-prone).
- The LLM-corrected Dresden TEI texts (1653–56, 1673) are "meaning-preserving" rather than character-exact, so forced alignment will mint noisier lines than from the 1665 text; the 12 + 20 ground-truth pages of the same manuscripts are the character-exact subset.
- The Christian II diaries are the closest structural analogue to Leibniz in the editions I found (mostly the prince's own hand, switching between German and, above all, French, Italian and Latin), but their licence and data format are unverified, so they are a lead rather than a resource.
- The Heidelberg edition's CC BY-SA 4.0 would impose ShareAlike on any redistributed derived text; its size (53 letters) makes it marginal for training.

### Gaps
- Kepler, Hevelius, Acta Pacis Westphalicae and the Rostock Matrikelportal were checked with a single search each, so everything about them is snippet-level; the "Briefe und Akten" series returned nothing.
- HAB edition: licence, TEI availability and IIIF links unverified (HTTP 503 for the edition, portal and PURL); a search snippet had mentioned "6,300 pages processed", which neither HAB page I read confirms.
- ECHO chapter on Liselotte's complete edition was blocked; its details are snippet-level.
- Whether Tübingen's Transkribus text is hand-corrected, and which other consilia volumes carry transcriptions, is not stated on the pages I read.
- No openly licensed Pietist letter transcriptions with images were found; one search for a Francke Foundations HTR or Transkribus project found none.

## 4. Is there openly available ground truth in Leibniz's own hand in German, or in the hands of his secretaries (Hodann, Eckhart)?

### Takeaway
I found none. The only open Leibniz-hand ground truth is PHILIUMM's Latin and French dataset (still CC BY 4.0), whose HTR and segmentation models turned CC BY-NC 4.0 on 2026-10-15; the GWLB Leibniz-Archiv's German letter transcriptions are CC BY-NC and are text, not aligned lines. No open dataset labels lines by scribe, so nothing identifies the hands of Hodann or Eckhart.

### Cited Findings
- **PHILIUMM dataset (details for the record).** Zenodo v1 (publication date 2026-07-27, record created and last modified 2026-09-10, CC BY 4.0, data.zip 4,175 MB): a manually corrected "clean" subset of 20,139 lines on 275 pages and an automatically aligned "noisy" subset of 43,372 lines on 735 pages, covering "philosophical writings, physics manuscripts, correspondence, drafts and clean copies" ([Zenodo 21622297](https://zenodo.org/records/21622297)). The Hugging Face card (resolves from DenisaB/ to DenisaBumba/htr_leibniz_dataset_v1) lists splits train_clean 18,254, train_noisy 43,372 and val 1,878 lines (27 files), languages Latin and French, noisy ground truth taken from Academy edition Series VI vol. 4 and aligned to FoNDUE-GD-v2 output, images from the GWLB IIIF service, and "no test set was created" ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/DenisaBumba/htr_leibniz_dataset_v1)).
- **Licence change on the models.** The PHILIUMM HTR model (Zenodo 21457538) and the baseline segmentation model (Zenodo 21537859) now show CC BY-NC 4.0, both records last modified 2026-10-15 ([HTR model](https://zenodo.org/records/21457538); [segmentation model](https://zenodo.org/records/21537859)). The model record reports CER 8.327, WER 28.557 after the clean pass and 15.044 / 48.882 for the FoNDUE-GD_v2 base (same HTR-model record). The project's own B1 report dated 2026-09-16 still calls the model CC BY 4.0 ([Zenodo 22782817](https://zenodo.org/records/22782817)); the Leibniz Legible README says the makers publish the model under CC BY-NC 4.0 since 2026-10-15, that the transcriptions stay CC BY by agreement, and that a model fine-tuned from the PHILIUMM checkpoint is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([README](https://github.com/marchofhares/leibnizlegible)). The dataset records I read still say CC BY 4.0 (Zenodo 21622297; HF card).
- **B1 reproduction (the project's own report, included for the record).** CER 7.95% (95% CI 7.49–8.46) against the claimed 8.33% on the model's 1,878-line validation split, WER 27.0% against 28.56%; zero-shot frontier vision-language models score 36.6% to 78.7% CER on those lines; "the validation split is Latin and French by construction, so German and Kurrent are unmeasured" ([Zenodo 22782817](https://zenodo.org/records/22782817)).
- **German side, from the project's own repository (not independent).** "The German stratum has no ground truth of its own"; the honest German test set is built from the GWLB Leibniz-Archiv's checked transcriptions of 1708–1716, CC BY-NC 4.0, for internal evaluation only ([README](https://github.com/marchofhares/leibnizlegible)). Its census finds 2,173 of 10,121 edition pieces (21.5%) German, 82 German pieces in Leibniz's own hand with 18,056 recognised lines, and 1,430 German pieces classed "other" hand ([census](https://github.com/marchofhares/LeibnizLegible/blob/d3391c2112bc099571aa11492d6d52d666fa338c/reports/kurrent/census.md)).
- **Secretaries.** Johann Friedrich Hodann: born 1674 in Braunschweig, died 1745 in Winsen (Luhe), servant to G. W. Leibniz in Hannover 1702–1714, rector in Winsen from 1715 ([Niedersächsische Personen](https://personen.niedersaechsische-bibliographie.de/person/export/1043103759/)). Johann Georg Eckhart: listed as Leibniz's secretary ([Niedersächsische Personen](https://personen.niedersaechsische-bibliographie.de/person/export/1043056416/)); other sources say Leibniz took him on as an assistant in 1694 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Georg_von_Eckhart), snippet only). None of these results mentions handwriting or any dataset.

### Inferences
- German training data in Leibniz's period has to come from other hands plus retro-alignment (the project's own route), because the open Leibniz-hand ground truth is Latin and French only.
- Since the census shows German pieces are mostly letters in correspondents' hands (1,430 "other" versus 82 "own"), diverse 1690–1716 correspondents' Kurrent matters more than secretary hands.
- The PHILIUMM clean set spans correspondence and clean copies, so it probably includes secretary-hand pages, but the records do not label hands; this is a guess.
- Models fine-tuned from the PHILIUMM checkpoint now inherit NC under the project's own policy; training from the dataset alone does not, as long as the dataset stays CC BY 4.0.

### Gaps
- No source found on digital scribe attribution for Hodann or Eckhart (the Kalliope database, suggested by a result, was not consulted).
- Whether the PHILIUMM dataset licence will also change is unknown; re-check the Zenodo and HF records before each release.

## 5. What are the licences and reuse conditions, and which datasets carry ShareAlike or NonCommercial clauses that would taint a trained model?

### Takeaway
Cleanly permissive sets that overlap the period or its edges are few: READ Bozen/ICFHR, READ Konzilsprotokolle, Königsfelden, FoNDUE-DE-MSS-18, mkn-kurrent-gt (Public Domain Mark) and CATMuS Modern. NonCommercial or ShareAlike problems attach to the Basel Urfehdenbuch (NC-SA), the three Dresden sets (CC BY in the metadata, CC BY-NC-SA in the descriptions), the Leopold I deposit (BY in the metadata, a BY-SA licence file, image rights with the archives), the Zürich Stillstandsprotokolle and Heidelberg letters (BY-SA), and the PHILIUMM models (BY-NC since 2026-10-15). Creative Commons' 2025 guide reads NC as covering every use that needs permission (training included) and BY/SA/ND as triggered when models or adaptations are publicly shared, and says that following it will almost certainly over-comply with copyright law.

### Cited Findings

#### 5A. Reference positions
- Creative Commons' guide "Using CC-licensed Works for AI Training" (May 2025) and its overview page say: CC licences "apply only when copyright permission is required", so where an exception or limitation applies the licence terms do not apply (the guide notes that Japan has an exception for training reproduction, the EU has one that rightsholders can opt out of, and Australia and the UK limit training broadly); BY, SA and ND conditions "are all triggered only upon public sharing of the original work or an adaptation of it", whereas NonCommercial "applies to all uses requiring permission under copyright", so it reaches training itself; on a conservative approach, a publicly shared model built on ShareAlike data "should be made available under the same CC license"; under NC, copying during training and later use or distribution of the trained model "would need to be for noncommercial purposes"; ND-restricted work should not be used as training data on the conservative approach, although the guide notes a model or its outputs would often not count as derivative works; and following the guidance "will almost certainly lead to overcompliance as a matter of copyright law" ([guide PDF](https://creativecommons.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Using-CC-licensed-Works-for-AI-Training.pdf); [overview page](https://creativecommons.org/using-cc-licensed-works-for-ai-training-2/)). The announcement post of 2025-05-15 adds that CC licence conditions "have limited application to machine reuse" ([primer post](https://creativecommons.org/2025/05/15/understanding-cc-licenses-and-ai-training-a-legal-primer/)). A University of Amsterdam IViR report on ShareAlike and machine learning also exists ([IViR PDF](https://ivir.nl/publicaties/download/Share-Alike-and-ML.pdf), title and listing only).
- The project's own policy: NC-licensed sources stay in a quarantined bucket excluded from CC BY releases, and "a model fine-tuned from the PHILIUMM checkpoint is CC BY-NC 4.0; one with no NC input CC BY 4.0" ([Leibniz Legible README](https://github.com/marchofhares/leibnizlegible)).

#### 5C. Observed mismatches between input licences and published model licences
- The Mannheim `german_handwriting` model is CC BY-SA 4.0, and its training list includes the Basel Urfehdenbuch X set that Zenodo labels CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([model](https://zenodo.org/records/7933463); [training list](https://github.com/UB-Mannheim/kraken/wiki/Training-German-Handwriting); [Basel set](https://zenodo.org/records/5153263)). A 2026 model derived from it is labelled CC BY 4.0 ([Zenodo 21747522](https://zenodo.org/records/21747522)).
- The Party base model is Apache-2.0, and its 148 listed sources include the Basel set (NC-SA per Zenodo) and the Incunabula Reichenau training data (Zenodo 11046062, labelled CC BY-SA 4.0 on its record) ([Party record](https://zenodo.org/records/20642057); [Incunabula record](https://zenodo.org/records/11046062)).
- `dh-unibe/trocr-kurrent-XVI-XVII` is MIT while its ground truth comes from several partner projects; the two `fgho` TrOCR models state no licence ([Bern card](https://huggingface.co/dh-unibe/trocr-kurrent-XVI-XVII); [fgho XVII](https://huggingface.co/fgho/trocr-hanseXVII-kurrent)).

#### 5D. Image rights differ from transcription rights
- SLUB scans: Public Domain Mark (Dresden records, above). GWLB scans: Public Domain Mark 1.0 ([Zenodo 22782817](https://zenodo.org/records/22782817)). Heidelberg facsimiles: "in the public domain" (terms page). Bavarian archive images: CC0 / Public Domain Mark (Gantner record). Leopold I images: rights remain with the archives (README). Basel images come from dokumente.stabs.ch with no rights statement in the Zenodo record.

### Inferences
- On the conservative reading (CC's guide plus the project's own policy), the NC bucket is: Dresden ×3, Basel Urfehdenbuch, GWLB letters, Francke material, the Bavarian part of Gantner, ADV-FORMS, the fgho plain-text set, and the PHILIUMM models (not the dataset). The SA bucket is: Leopold I (if LICENSE.MD governs), Zürich TEI, Heidelberg, Bullinger and Semper fair copies.
- A permissive-only in-period pool is therefore small: READ Bozen and Königsfelden (both with unknown in-period share) plus the Hanse sets if their MIT tags are honoured and the archive image rights allow it.
- The Hugging Face "MIT" tags on aggregated archival data cannot be relied on as a rights statement: the cards give no basis for them, and a Bullinger-related letters set is tagged MIT while the Bullinger HTR dataset is CC BY-SA (whether the former derives from the latter is not stated).
- Practice in the field is looser than the conservative reading (the Party and Mannheim examples), which shows what others do, not what the law requires; whether ShareAlike reaches model weights is unsettled and I cannot resolve it here.

### Gaps
- No legal opinion was sought; jurisdiction-specific text-and-data-mining exceptions were not examined.
- The Dresden author's intended licence (BY or NC-SA) is not stated consistently anywhere I looked.
- Which upstream set supplies CATMuS Modern's 1,940 German lines is not stated on the card (FoNDUE-DE-MSS-18 has 2,069 lines and may be the source; unverified).

## 6. What exists but is NOT open (so the project knows what it cannot use)?

### Takeaway
Every Transkribus public model for early modern Kurrent that I checked publishes page, line and word counts and a validation CER, but no licence and no statement that the training data is public. The largest are German Giant I (86,345 pages) and Early Kurrent Emperor I (29,081 pages); only some related ground truth exists as open downloads (Greifswald's 8,770-line READ set, the Königsfelden data, and now the Hanse sets on Hugging Face).

### Cited Findings
- **The German Giant I** (ID 50870, PyLaia, published 2023-03-20, Kurrent and Latin script including Sütterlin, 16th–21st c.): 86,345 pages, 2,771,772 lines, 15,420,976 words; validation CER 8.3%; data availability not stated; no licence beyond Transkribus terms; the page advises against using it as a base model for custom training ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/the-german-giant-i)).
- **Transkribus German Kurrent** (ID 36508, 2021-09-11, Kurrent, Sütterlin and Fraktur, 17th–20th c.): 16,247 pages, 516,342 lines, 3,209,689 words; CER 5.4%; built from German Kurrent M1, German Kurrent 17th and 18th C. and smaller sets ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/german-kurrent-and-sutterlin-17th-20th-century)).
- **German_Kurrent_17th-18th** (ID 27457, University of Greifswald, 2020-11-09): 8,915 pages, 289,857 lines, 1,839,841 words; CER 5.5%; about 1,500 handwriters from Greifswald council minutes, Wismar High Court assessor votes, Stralsund government minutes, Greifswald Law Faculty responsa and private letter collections; data availability not stated ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/german-kurrent-17th-18th-century)). Greifswald's own announcement (2021-01-25) speaks of about 2000 individual writers' hands (the Transkribus page says about 1,500), with about 35% of the ground truth from the 17th century, about 50% from the 18th and the rest from the decades around them; mainly official minutes and legal documents plus private records and letters, mostly German and Latin with some Low German and French; it does not say whether the ground truth is published ([Greifswald blog](https://rechtsprechung-im-ostseeraum.archiv.uni-greifswald.de/?p=1595)).
- **Acta_17 PyLaia (German, Latin)** (ID 27337, published 2020-11-04, 1580–1705): 3,657 training pages, 102,545 lines, 594,628 words; CER 5.8%; more than 500,000 words from about 1,000 writers, based on legal texts and court writings from the Responsa of the Greifswald Law Faculty; ground truth by Dirk Alvermann, Elisabeth Heigl and Anna Brandt; training data public: not stated ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/german-lower-german-latin-17th-century)).
- **Transkribus Early Kurrent M1** (ID 37789, 2021-10-26, 16th–early 19th c.): 5,854 pages, 186,520 lines, 1,096,690 words; CER 6.3% ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/transkribus-early-kurrent-m1)). **Early Kurrent Emperor I** (ID 50856, 2023-03-20): 29,081 pages, 1,095,751 lines, 6,404,094 words; CER 8% ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/early-kurrent-emperor-i)). Both pages say "a diverse dataset" without naming sources; neither states whether the data is public.
- **German Kurrent 16th–18th**: CER 8.42% on about 1.58 million words of Swiss-weighted ground truth (Zürich State Archives, Königsfelden, Einsiedeln) ([Transkribus page](https://www.transkribus.org/model/german-kurrent-16th-18th), from a search snippet; my fetch of the page returned 404).
- **Hanserezesse 17th Century v.5**: see section 2B; its page does not say the data is public ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/hanserezesse-17th-century-v5)).
- Open data next to closed models: the only openly published Greifswald ground truth I found is the 8,770-line READ set (late 18th c.), against 289,857 lines in the Greifswald model, and whether the former is part of the latter is not stated ([Zenodo 215383](https://zenodo.org/records/215383); [Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/german-kurrent-17th-18th-century)); Bern's TrOCR was trained on about 1,579,200 words from partner projects, of which only some (Königsfelden) are published as datasets ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/dh-unibe/trocr-kurrent-XVI-XVII)).

### Inferences
- Treat these models as baselines to compare against, not as data sources; there is no route to their training data other than the originating projects' own releases (Greifswald, Hanse, Bern).
- The scale gap between private ground truth (German Giant 86,345 pages; Greifswald 8,915; Early Kurrent M1 5,854) and verified open in-period ground truth (about 1.6 thousand pages) explains why public Transkribus models may outperform what open data alone can reproduce.

### Gaps
- Transkribus model pages never state whether training data may be redistributed; I did not contact the model authors.

## 7. How relevant is each resource to Leibniz's hand (a scholar's cursive with many abbreviations, heavy revisions and German, Latin and French on one page)?

### Takeaway
No open dataset matches Leibniz's hand. The nearest structural analogues are individual-hand sources with mixed scripts (Leopold I's autograph letters, the Tübingen Konsilien, Zürich pastors' notes, and, if its terms allow, Christian II's diaries); the large chancery sets (Dresden, Hanse, Bozen) teach generic Kurrent letterforms and Early Modern German spelling but not revision-heavy scholarly cursive.

### Cited Findings
- Facts the judgements rest on, all cited above: Leopold I letters are "German kurrent and Latin cursive" in a script described as "difficult to read", single hand, abbreviations quietly resolved ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HTR-School-Vienna/2025--early-modern-german/main/README.md)); Dresden is "typical late 17th century Saxon kurrent ('Kanzleikurrent')" ([Zenodo 15303398](https://zenodo.org/records/15303398)); Zürich protocols are "fleeting, hard-to-read notes in the individual hand of the pastor" ([Zenodo 3985144](https://zenodo.org/records/3985144)); Tübingen's text mixes German and Latin on the page ([OpenDigi text view](https://opendigi.ub.uni-tuebingen.de/opendigi/UAT_084_13/ocr)); the Hanse sets are administrative recesses with Middle Low German and New High German ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fgho/hanse-kurrent-xvi-lines)); Christian II's diarist switches between German and "above all French, Italian and Latin" ([idw press release](https://idw-online.de/en/news628178)); the PHILIUMM set is Latin and French ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/DenisaBumba/htr_leibniz_dataset_v1)).
- The project's own smoke test ranked readers on 383 lines of Dresden K.117: the Bern TrOCR XVI–XVII model 7.4% CER, the Hanse XVI and XVII TrOCR models 8.9% and 10.9%, the PHILIUMM kraken model 29.5%, McCATMuS 51.7%; the project notes the Dresden hand is a chancery's, "not Leibniz's or his correspondents'" ([project report](https://github.com/marchofhares/LeibnizLegible/blob/d3391c2112bc099571aa11492d6d52d666fa338c/reports/kurrent/bootstrap-candidates.md)).

### Inferences
*(These are my judgements. I inspected no page images and ran no experiments; the only measured figures are the project's own, quoted above.)*

1. **PHILIUMM dataset: right hand, wrong language.** High relevance for Leibniz's letterforms, abbreviations and revision layout; it lacks German words and orthography (Latin and French only, German unmeasured).
2. **Leopold I letters: medium to high for mixed-script cursive, medium overall.** Private autograph letters in a hard, individual hand, with German Kurrent and Latin cursive in the same corpus (the README does not say per page), are the best structural match found; but the period (1655–1667) precedes Leibniz's German letters, abbreviations are resolved silently (so line text is not diplomatic), 271 pages from one hand risks overfitting, and image rights sit with the archives.
3. **Tübingen Konsilien 1659–1665: medium, potentially large.** Scribe-written legal opinions (the writer is not identified in what I read) with German and Latin mixed in the text, 553 canvases, public domain, but transcription quality is undocumented and alignment work is needed.
4. **Zürich Stillstandsprotokolle: medium for hand, low to medium for language.** Individual educated pastors' quick notes are closer to non-chancery cursive than chancery hands, but Swiss usage, SA licence and external images apply.
5. **Christian II diaries: closest analogue in kind, but a lead only.** Mostly one educated writer's own hand across German, French, Italian and Latin; licence, format and access were not verified.
6. **Hanse 17th-century sets: low for hand, medium for volume.** The largest in-period pool (1,298 page rows) but formal chancery writing and political-legal vocabulary; useful to teach general Kurrent forms if the rights question is settled.
7. **Dresden diaries: low to medium.** Regular Saxon chancery script; best used as a character-exact evaluation set (42 pages) rather than as a Leibniz proxy, and NC-SA applies on the conservative reading.
8. **READ Bozen, Konzilsprotokolle, mkn-kurrent-gt, FoNDUE-DE-MSS-18: low to medium.** Council, university and copyist hands from a longer or later period (only FoNDUE-DE-MSS-18 sits in the first half of the 18th century), mostly small or of unknown in-period share; clean licences are their main value.
9. **19th-century Kurrent sets: low.** Useful only for script pre-training; letterforms and spelling differ from 1690–1716.
10. **German pieces in the Academy edition are mostly other people's hands** (1,430 "other" versus 82 "own" pieces, per the project's census), so diversity of 1690–1716 correspondents matters more than imitating Leibniz's own German hand.

### Gaps
- Hand similarity was not measured; no page images were opened.
- I have no data on Leibniz's own German hand beyond the project's census, and no open source labelling Hodann's or Eckhart's hands.
