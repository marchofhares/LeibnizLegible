# Latin and French early-modern HTR: open ground truth, base models and data-scaling evidence for Leibniz's hand (state of play, 26 October 2026)

*Method note.* Snapshot taken 26 Oct 2026. "Catalogue" = the HTR-United `catalog.json` (138 entries) fetched that day: [catalog.json](https://htr-united.github.io/htr-united/catalog.json). Kraken model records on Zenodo store a validation "accuracy" (character accuracy on that model's *own* validation split, see [kraken training docs](https://kraken.re/7.1/user_guide/training_recognition.html)); where I write "CER = 100 − accuracy" the CER is my arithmetic and is **not comparable across models**. Items marked *Estimate* are my own derivations from cited numbers. Where a page could not be opened I say so.

## Q1. What do HTR-United and related repositories offer for Latin and French handwriting c. 1550–1800 (licences, sizes, hands, formats), and which are diplomatic vs normalised?

### Takeaway
Open Latin/French handwriting ground truth for 1550–1800 is small and fragmented: about 20.5k lines of French 1600–1800 in the HTR-United catalogue proper (about 58k if the unlisted Basel "Crimes et châtiments" sets are added), a few thousand lines of 16th-c. Latin (Gwalther, Sardi), plus one very large but edition-aligned, CC BY-SA set (Bullinger, 165,673 lines, 16th c.). Almost all the French 17th–18th-c. sets are already inside the FoNDUE-GD base the project uses. The datasets divide into a diplomatic family (abbreviations kept: CREMMA, FoNDUE, Lectaurep, HTRomance, CATMuS) and an edition-style family (abbreviations resolved: Bullinger, Gwalther, TRIDIS, Königsfelden, Titres nobiliaires).

### Cited Findings
**Catalogue scope**
- The catalogue snapshot has 138 entries; by my count 37 are handwriting-containing entries overlapping 1550–1800 in any language — [catalog.json](https://htr-united.github.io/htr-united/catalog.json).
- There is **no "CREMMA Lettres"** entry. CREMMA entries present are: Medieval, Medii Aevi (Latin), MSS 16/17/18/19/20, Early Modern Books (print), Wikipedia, AN-Testament de Poilus — [catalog.json](https://htr-united.github.io/htr-united/catalog.json). Gallicorpora entries are printed books (16e/17e/18e s.) plus 15th-c. manuscripts/incunables, i.e. no Gallicorpora handwriting for 1550–1800 — [catalog.json](https://htr-united.github.io/htr-united/catalog.json).
- Lectaurep entries: Répertoires (1830–1939, 29,410 lines), Mariages et divorces (1829–1928, 20,304 lines) and the 18th-c. Bronod registers (below) — [catalog.json](https://htr-united.github.io/htr-united/catalog.json). No "Hugo Schuchardt" or other Latin-letter collection appears.

**Conventions (diplomatic vs normalised)**
- Diplomatic/graphemic, abbreviations kept: CREMMA-MSS-16/17/18 ("Abréviations conservées"), Bronod, Château de Chavigny, Sardi, CREMMA Medii Aevi, ARletta, Revolutionary City — all via [catalog.json](https://htr-united.github.io/htr-united/catalog.json). CATMuS Medieval: "strictly graphematic transcriptions. No abbreviations are resolved", NFD — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/15030337).
- Edition-style, abbreviations resolved: TRIDIS ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.22714)), Titres nobiliaires, Bullinger (HBBW edition text), Gwalther (transcriptions by P. Stotz; the catalogue does not state their conventions, so "edition-style" is my inference) — [catalog.json](https://htr-united.github.io/htr-united/catalog.json). For Bullinger, the later alignment paper notes that expert annotations for historians are regularised texts with resolved abbreviations and normalised special characters, and that its algorithm cannot yet handle abbreviations — [Peer et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07904). The CATMuS paper states that "solely edition-based datasets present normalized punctuation, spacing, and capitals, as well as resolved abbreviation" — [CATMuS Medieval, HAL](https://inria.hal.science/hal-04453952).
- Mixing evidence: a French 17th-c. Transkribus project concluded that homogeneity of transcription rules is essential and that failing to ensure it "presents a risk of misleading the model, especially with abbreviations" — [Couture et al., JDMDH](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11146). The FoNDUE-GD authors report that "the heterogeneity of the transcription practices seems to have a minimal impact" for their massive model but name harmonisation "the next challenge" — [Gabay et al., DH2025](https://hal.science/hal-05063299/document). PP-OCRv6's card warns that mixing CATMuS and TRIDIS "have very different approaches to transcription" so "inconsistent output is to be expected" — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/21788405). The FoNDUE-GD pipeline harmonised characters with Chocomufin (e.g. straight vs curly quotes) — [Gabay et al.](https://hal.science/hal-05063299/document).

**What the project's base (FoNDUE-GD) already contains**
- Training total 20,761 pages / 660,496 lines: Dutch 306,207 lines (IJSBERG/VOC 259,517 + REPUBLIC 46,690), German 164,986, French 168,848, Latin 15,052, Spanish 5,403. French 17th–18th c. inside it: FoNDUE-FR-MSS-18 8,496; MEGV 7,566; CREMMA-MSS-18 4,019; Bronod 3,707; CREMMA-MSS-17 2,213; FoNDUE-FR-MSS-17 91; AAEB "Crimes et châtiments" 17th c. 22,359 and 16th c. 14,856; HTRomance 3,385. Latin: PHILIUMM 13,049 and Sardi 2,003 lines — [FoNDUE-GD record 2.1 (24 Jul 2026)](https://zenodo.org/records/21536798), [DH2025 paper Table 1](https://hal.science/hal-05063299/document). The v2.1 record lists the same file size (16,310,798 bytes) as the Dec-2024 v2 record — [v2](https://zenodo.org/records/14399779).
- Not in that list: Bullinger, Gwalther, TRIDIS, the Revolutionary City corpus, ARletta, Titres nobiliaires, Calvin, Mémoire sur St Domingue — [FoNDUE-GD record](https://zenodo.org/records/21536798).

### Inferences
- Summing the French 1600–1800 sets listed above (CREMMA-17 2,245 + CREMMA-18 4,019 + FoNDUE-18 6,446 + HTRomance 3,386 + Bronod 3,702 + Titres 726) gives about 20.5k lines; with AAEB French 16th–17th c. (37,215 lines) about 58k. *Estimate* (some HTRomance pages are 16th/19th c.). The project's single-author corpus (about 63k clean+noisy PHILIUMM lines, plus roughly 297k aligned lines) is therefore larger than the entire open French 1600–1800 handwriting pool.
- No Latin handwriting dataset in the catalogue snapshot falls in 1600–1800; the Latin entries stop at about 1570–1600 (Gwalther, Sardi, Bullinger, Königsfelden, CREMMA Medii Aevi) and TRIDIS ends in the early 17th century. The only 17th-c. Latin cursive in the FoNDUE-GD list is the Leibniz material itself.
- Re-adding the French HTR-United sets to a FoNDUE-GD-derived fine-tune is largely duplication; the untapped open pools are 16th-c. Latin (Bullinger, Gwalther, TRIDIS) and non-French script-close material, and these sit a century or more before Leibniz and use different conventions.
- The Academy-edition aligned lines resemble the *edition-style* family (resolved abbreviations, no diplomatic marking), so TRIDIS/Bullinger/Gwalther are closer in convention than CREMMA/CATMuS-type data (assumes the Academy reading text expands abbreviations, which I did not verify).

### Gaps
- FONDUE-FR-MSS-17, FONDUE-LA-MSS-16-PR/17-PR and FONDUE-FR-AAEB-16/17 repositories could not be opened in this environment (raw README 404, github.com pages 403), so their licences and image availability are unverified; only the FoNDUE-GD record's "Yes/Partially" is available.
- Hand counts per dataset are mostly "estimated" or "unknown" in the catalogue; no dataset gives a breakdown by script type (secretary/italic/mixta).
- ARletta's French share is not stated in the sources I read.

## Q2. Which open base models report the lowest CER on 17th-century Latin/French cursive, and are they compatible with kraken 7 (format, codec)?

### Takeaway
No open model reports a CER specifically on 17th-century Latin/French scholarly cursive. The only such measurement is on the Leibniz validation split: FoNDUE-GD zero-shot 15.04% CER, 8.33% after two-stage fine-tuning (7.95% on independent reproduction). The strongest generic candidates are new kraken-ecosystem models: PP-OCRv6 (Apache-2.0; needs kraken ≥ 7.1; Latin 6.45% / French 7.56% CER for the medium size on a random in-domain split) and Party (Apache-2.0; Latin 7.47%, French 7.49%). The `.mlmodel` family (FoNDUE-GD, McCATMuS, Manu McFondue, CATMuS Medieval, TRIDIS v2) loads in kraken 7.x.

### Cited Findings
**Reference point: the Leibniz model**
- PHILIUMM's model is FoNDUE-GD_v2 fine-tuned in two phases (43,372 noisy aligned lines, lr 2e-4; then 20,139 clean lines, lr 5e-5); on 27 validation files: base CER 15.044 / WER 48.882 → 10.864 / 34.493 after the noisy pass → 8.327 / 28.557 after the clean pass. Licence **CC BY-NC 4.0** — [Zenodo 21457538](https://zenodo.org/records/21457538).
- Independent reproduction: 7.95% CER (95% CI 7.49–8.46), WER 27.0%, on the model's 1,878-line validation split; zero-shot frontier VLMs 36.6–78.7% CER on the same lines; the split is Latin/French only and drawn from the manually corrected subset, so it is a line-level figure on pre-segmented lines — [Leibniz Legible Phase B1](https://zenodo.org/records/22782817).

**New generation (kraken ≥ 7.1, released 5 Aug 2026)**
- Kraken 7.1 added the PP-OCRv6 architecture with pretrained multilingual base models (tiny/small/medium) "trained on a diverse corpus … essentially the entirety of openly accessible historical ATR datasets" plus private and synthetic data — [kraken changelog](https://kraken.re/7.0/changelog.html). Models are Apache-2.0 safetensors: [tiny](https://zenodo.org/records/21788403) (2.8 MB), [small](https://zenodo.org/records/21788405) (13 MB, ~3.24M parameters), [medium](https://zenodo.org/records/21788410) (64 MB); parameter counts 0.68 / 3.22 / 15.86 M — [Kiessling 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20064).
- Per-language CER (tiny / small / medium) on a held-out random 5% split capped at 100 pages per language, **not document-disjoint** ("best read as in-domain generalization"): Latin 14.03 / 9.12 / 6.45; French 14.02 / 10.01 / 7.56; Middle French 8.70 / 5.03 / 3.41; Italian 7.22 / 3.90 / 2.89; Spanish 6.19 / 5.00 / 4.49; Dutch 14.31 / 8.97 / 6.86; micro-average 8.71 / 5.43 / 3.91 — [kraken changelog](https://kraken.re/7.0/changelog.html), [Zenodo 21788405 README](https://zenodo.org/records/21788405).
- Its published training list names, among early-modern sets, FONDUE-FR-MSS-18, FONDUE-FR-AAEB-16/-17, FONDUE-LA-MSS-16-PR and -17-PR, FONDUE-NE-MSS-17-PR, TitresNobiliaires_17_18, Mémoire sur St Domingue, HTRomance modern, EpiSearch, LiDi, Sloane Lab, Revolutionary City, ARletta and VOC; I did not find CREMMA-MSS-17/18 or the Bronod registers in it. The card states "the corpus mixes conventions" — [Zenodo 21788405](https://zenodo.org/records/21788405).
- Usage: `ketos train --arch ppocrv6`; "a general-purpose model is available for each variant and can be used as a starting point for fine-tuning"; PP-OCRv6 "cannot be used with older versions" than 7.1; fine-tune with `--load`, codec mismatch handled by `--resize union|new|fail`, `--freeze-backbone N` against forgetting — [kraken 7.1 training docs](https://kraken.re/7.1/user_guide/training_recognition.html).
- Party (Kiessling): page-wise generative recognizer (Swin encoder + small Llama decoder, UTF-8 byte tokens), Apache-2.0, prompted with kraken line positions, "intended to be fine-tuned"; held-out CER by language Latin 7.47, French 7.49, Middle French 1.47, Italian 2.81, Spanish 6.07, Dutch 8.14, German 9.10, English 7.63 on test splits from the training sources; weights 518 MB — [Zenodo 20642057](https://zenodo.org/records/20642057); earlier European-languages version (858 MB, no metrics filled in) — [Zenodo 15764161](https://zenodo.org/records/15764161).

**Compatibility with kraken 7**
- Kraken 7 makes safetensors the default output; `weights_format: coreml` in the experiment YAML saves a model "with kraken < 7 compatibility" — [kraken 7.1 docs](https://kraken.re/7.1/user_guide/training_recognition.html). Kraken's model docs still show `.mlmodel` files used for inference, and 7.0.3 fixed a CoreML-loader bug for pre-1.0 models — [models page](https://kraken.re/7.1/user_guide/models.html), [changelog](https://kraken.re/7.0/changelog.html). 7.0.3 also makes `ketos test` apply a chosen Unicode normalisation to both prediction and ground truth — [changelog](https://kraken.re/7.0/changelog.html).
- Codec: FoNDUE-GD, McCATMuS, CATMuS Medieval and Manu McFondue are NFD-trained (embedded codec of roughly 216 entries for the generic FoNDUE-GD model and 365 for its language variants — my extraction, taken from the highest code index in the model files); TRIDIS v2 has normalisation "None" — [Zenodo 14399779](https://zenodo.org/records/14399779), [13788177](https://zenodo.org/records/13788177), [13862096](https://zenodo.org/records/13862096).

**Comparative evidence across architectures (Kiessling, 17 Sep 2026 preprint)**
- Generalised models on a 33-language held-out set (micro CER): kraken CRNN 10.84; PP-OCRv6 tiny 14.25, small 9.33, medium 7.03 — [arXiv 2609.20064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20064).
- After fine-tuning on CATMuS Medieval, CER on ICDAR 2026 CMMHWR test sets (CRNN vs PP-OCRv6 medium): French 5.01 vs 3.36; Latin 12.61 vs 8.70; Spanish 10.47 vs 7.99; CoMMA Latin 13.48 vs 11.30; CoMMA French 21.65 vs 21.36 — [arXiv 2609.20064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20064). Trained from scratch on the full CATMuS corpus the CRNN was as good or better (French 4.32 vs 4.94 medium; Latin 11.00 vs 12.17) — same paper.
- In the same paper a fine-tuned PP-OCRv6 medium beat the Qwen3.5-9B-based Medusa line recognizer on Latin (8.70 vs 13.07) — [arXiv 2609.20064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20064).

**Other engines (not kraken-loadable)**
- TrOCR: TRIDIS TrOCR (MIT, medieval/early-modern semi-diplomatic; "CER of about 0.05 to 0.10 on three external unseen datasets") — [HF](https://huggingface.co/magistermilitum/tridis_v2_HTR_historical_manuscripts); `dh-unibe/trocr-medieval-escriptmask` (MIT, eval CER 0.0329, medieval charters) — [HF](https://huggingface.co/dh-unibe/trocr-medieval-escriptmask); `medieval-data/trocr-medieval-*` (MIT; "not formally tested") — [example](https://huggingface.co/medieval-data/trocr-medieval-humanistica).
- Teklia's PyLaia models on HF (MIT) are named after Belfort, POPP, Himanis, Home-Alcar, Esposalles, NorHand, IAM, RIMES and similar corpora; none is named for 1600–1800 French or Latin — [HF model search](https://huggingface.co/api/models?search=pylaia&limit=20).
- Loghi: suggested starting model `generic-2023-02-15`, "should work ok on 17th and 18th century handwritten dutch"; hosted on SURFdrive; the README states no licence for it — [Loghi README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/knaw-huc/loghi/main/README.md).
- Churro-3B (Qwen2.5-VL-3B fine-tune, 99,491 pages, 46 language clusters): 70.1% normalised-Levenshtein similarity on handwritten text; HF licence tag "other" — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19768), [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/stanford-oval/churro-3B).
- The FoNDUE-GD authors excluded LLM/VLM options because they were paid and not (yet) deployable in sealed environments — [Gabay et al.](https://hal.science/hal-05063299/document).

### Inferences
- Model figures in the table are validation scores on each model's own mix and cannot rank the models for Leibniz; the only fair comparison is to score each candidate zero-shot and after identical fine-tuning on the frozen Leibniz validation split (`ketos test` with NFD normalisation, as the PP-OCRv6 card does).
- PP-OCRv6 is the best-documented route to a stronger base: it is the family with the clearest published matched-fine-tuning gains over the kraken CRNN that I found (about −24% to −33% relative CER on Latin/French/Spanish test manuscripts; calculation from the numbers above), it is small (a 15.9M-parameter medium model; fine-tuning on a 6 GB card is plausible but unsourced), and it needs kraken 7.1 (the project runs 7.0.3).
- Candidates worth a zero-shot and a matched fine-tune on the frozen validation split: PP-OCRv6 medium and small (kraken ≥ 7.1), Party-pretrained (separate `party` package), the FoNDUE-GD language variants (their own validation CER was lower than the generic model's: Latin 8.58, French 7.64 vs 8.90), and McCATMuS for French (it has no handwritten Latin).
- Its Latin and French headline CERs are dominated by medieval and 19th–20th-c. material (its French list is mostly 19th–20th-c. and print), so they say little about 1680–1716 cursive.
- The project's single-hand data (63k clean+noisy lines, plus the aligned lines) is far larger than the few-shot and few-page regimes in which most base-model gains were measured; at that scale the architecture should matter less (the CRNN equalled or beat PP-OCRv6 when trained from scratch on about 142k CATMuS lines), so a smaller gain than the 24–33% above is the reasonable expectation. *Estimate; no direct measurement.*

### Gaps
- No open model reports CER on 17th-century Latin/French scholarly cursive; no published PP-OCRv6 or Party fine-tune on a single writer with ≥1,000 pages was found. Kiessling's few-shot figure (20–240 lines on four manuscripts) is only in a chart, not text.
- Party's memory footprint for fine-tuning on 6 GB is not documented.
- Whether the Leibniz validation files overlap FoNDUE-GD's training: the dataset card says part of the clean data "was later used to train FoNDUE-GD-v2" ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/DenisaBumba/htr_leibniz_dataset_v1)) and FoNDUE-GD 2.1 lists 13,049 PHILIUMM lines, yet the model card reports 15.04% zero-shot CER; exclusion of the 27 validation files is not stated.
- Churro and Medusa licences/weights were not verified beyond the HF tag.

## Q3. Are there open datasets of scholars' Latin/French letters 1650–1720 (Hevelius, Kircher, Boyle, Huygens, Bernoulli, Royal Society "Science in the Making", Oldenburg) usable for alignment-based ground truth?

### Takeaway
No ready-made, openly licensed, image-aligned ground truth for Leibniz-era scholarly letters was found. The partial candidates are restricted: BEOL/Bernoulli (project default licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), Newton Project (transcriptions CC BY-NC-ND 3.0), Huygens manuscripts (Brill paywall), Kircher (no licence found). The open material is 16th-century (Bullinger, Gwalther) or French samples c. 1700 in CREMMA-MSS-17 (Montfaucon correspondence). Bullinger's text-to-image alignment is the methodological precedent for the project's retro-alignment.

### Cited Findings
- **BEOL (Bernoulli-Euler Online, Basel/DaSCH, shortcode 0801)**: integrates the Bernoulli correspondence edition (BEBB) and Euler's *Opera Omnia* IVA/IV, "currently connected to the repositories of The Newton Project and the Briefportal Leibniz"; keywords include Leibniz, Newton, Euler, Goldbach; project-level `dataLicense` is `cc-by-nc-sa-4.0` and the enabled licences include CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC, CC BY-ND, CC BY-NC-ND and public domain, so licences are per resource — [DaSCH project API](https://api.dasch.swiss/admin/projects/shortcode/0801). The BEBB project page lists Daniel, Jacob II, Johann I, Johann II, Nicolaus I and II Bernoulli and Jacob Hermann as correspondents, with "searchable edited texts linked with facsimiles"; Leibniz is not named on that page — [BEBB at DaSCH](https://dasch.swiss/projects/0801d/). A search summary of the DaSCH record gives CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for BEBB (dated 2021-09-02) — [DaSCH repository record](https://repository.dasch.swiss/dpe/projects/0801d). The Euler–Goldbach edition converts transcriptions to TEI/XML — [DH2018 abstract](https://dh2018.adho.org/a-digital-edition-of-leonhard-eulers-correspondence-with-christian-goldbach/).
- **Newton Project**: sample transcribed letters 1672–1675 carry CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 (text) and CC BY-NC 3.0 (downloadable images), publisher University of Sussex — e.g. [NATP00242](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/xml/NATP00242), [NATP00264](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/xml/NATP00264) (based on a sample, not the whole site).
- **Royal Society "Science in the Making"**: free-access platform with 30,000+ digitised archival items — [History of Technology notice](https://www.historyoftechnology.org/2023/07/03/royl-society-publishing-launched-new-free-access-science-platform/); IIIF and W3C Web Annotation — [TDL abstract](https://tdl-ir.tdl.org/items/4a03847d-fcaf-4b21-9bb5-55b964e3b2da). A transcription page exists for a Hevelius→Oldenburg letter, shelfmark EL/H2/3 — [item](https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/el_h2_3/transcription-of-a-letter-from-johannes-hevelius-dated-at-danzig-gdansk-to-henry-oldenburg) (found via search; page itself returned HTTP 503 here).
- **Hevelius**: a critical edition is in progress at Brepols (vol. 4, correspondence with Lubieniecki, 2021, French and Latin; 2,000+ letters from about 430 correspondents) and Electronic Enlightenment holds Hevelius letters but is subscriber-only — both taken from search summaries, pages not opened: [library record](https://hds.hebis.de/herder/Record/HEB487268601), [Oxford CofK post](https://cofk.history.ox.ac.uk/tag/johannes-hevelius/index.html).
- **Kircher**: Stanford's facsimile edition of the letters requires the Luna Insight viewer — [Stanford](https://web.stanford.edu/group/kircher/); the Pontifical Gregorian University's GATE wiki runs volunteer transcription of 14 volumes; counts conflict (metadata for 264 of 2,623 letters vs 2,739 letters in the EMLO collaboration) and no open licence or bulk download was found — [GATE AKC page](https://gate.unigre.it/mediawiki/index.php/Athanasius_Kircher_Correspondence_(AKC)).
- **Huygens**: the digitised archive is Brill's *Codices Hugeniani Online*; free access only for Leiden accounts — [Brill](https://primarysources.brillonline.com/browse/codices-hugeniani). A single scan of Constantijn Huygens letters (BPL 2746) is on Wikimedia Commons under CC BY 4.0 — [Commons file](https://download.osmand.net/wiki/File:BPL_2746.pdf) (as reported by search; not opened).
- **Boyle / Oldenburg**: nothing open with images and transcriptions was found besides the Making Science items above.
- **Open scholarly French correspondence c. 1700**: CREMMA-MSS-17 includes Dom Bernard de Montfaucon's correspondence (two volumes, 49 of 111 files) with Gallica images, CC BY 4.0, abbreviations kept — [repo](https://github.com/HTR-United/CREMMA-MSS-17).
- **16th-c. scholarly Latin letters (open)**: Bullinger HTR dataset (CC BY-SA 4.0, 165,673 lines) and Gwalther (CC BY 4.0, 4,040 lines) — see Q1. The Bullinger set was produced by aligning edition text to lines with Transkribus Text2Image and warns of residual errors — [repo](https://github.com/pstroe/bullinger-htr).
- **Method precedent and refinements**: errors from automatic alignment (wrong hyphenation, missing/extra words) are systematic; a CTC-alignment self-training method improved PyLaia by 1.1 points CER and "weaker models yield more accurate alignments" (line-level alignment accuracy 88.5% vs 86.1% on subset 1; 89.1% vs 77.3% on subset 2) — [Peer et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07904). The project's own report describes retro-alignment of copyright-expired Akademie-Ausgabe reading text, 297,424 minted lines, with the precision audit unfinished — [Leibniz Legible B2–C2](https://zenodo.org/records/22782819).

### Inferences
- For Leibniz-era hands, the realistic alignment sources are public-domain printed editions (as the project already does for the Academy edition). None of the open image+text collections found covers 1650–1720 Latin scholars in a licence-clean, bulk-downloadable way.
- BEOL is the most relevant structured source (Bernoulli, Euler, Goldbach, links to Leibniz and Newton repositories), but its default NC-SA licence and per-resource licensing would constrain a CC BY training release.

### Gaps
- Science in the Making's terms of use could not be read (HTTP 503; TLS failure with curl), so reuse rights and bulk access (IIIF) are unverified.
- BEBB's per-letter licences, language mix and number of letters were not retrievable; the DaSCH page fetch did not display the licence (taken from search summary and the project API default).
- Huygens *Oeuvres complètes* (1888–1950) public-domain scans as an alignment text source were not checked; likewise any Leeuwenhoek, Mersenne, Peiresc, Boulliau or Wallis editions.
- Electronic Enlightenment's terms and the Wikimedia Commons file were reported only through search summaries.

## Q4. What gains do published studies report from out-of-domain Latin/French ground truth, more in-domain data, or a better base for one writer's model at about 8% CER?

### Takeaway
The closest published analogues say: (i) a stronger/pretrained base gives large, reliable gains, especially on new material (−24% to −33% relative CER vs the kraken CRNN after matched CATMuS fine-tuning, −35% zero-shot on the 33-language test; up to −66% in a small-data Transkribus case) — but a larger base with the wrong lexicon can lose to a smaller matched one; (ii) once the writer is in the training data, extra writer-specific training gives small relative gains (about 4–15%), and in the few-pages regime each doubling of data cut CER by roughly 11–21% with shrinking returns; (iii) out-of-domain data helps mainly through pretraining and when text/lexicon match, and more heterogeneous data can degrade the best manuscripts; (iv) noisy alignment labels distort evaluation, and filtering flagged lines did not help in Bullinger. No study isolates the 8% → 5% step for a single early-modern writer with about 60k lines.

### Cited Findings
**The project's own ablation (more in-domain data, noisy vs clean)**
- Base 15.044% → 10.864% after 43,372 noisy aligned lines → 8.327% after 20,139 clean lines (CER on 27 validation files); "adding slightly noisy automatically aligned data proved to be very effective" — [Zenodo 21457538](https://zenodo.org/records/21457538).

**Additional data and fine-tuning, writer in or out of the training set (closest published analogues to the project's situation)**
- Bullinger writer-adaptation benchmark (16th-c. Latin/German letters): generic PyLaia 8.36% CER on 106 frequent writers (HTR-Flor 9.56%) and 9.85% on 200 non-frequent writers; continuing training on a frequent writer improved PyLaia CER by 3.70% relative (8.36 → 8.05; 84/106 writers improved) and HTR-Flor by 9.76% (9.56 → 8.62); restarting with a high learning rate was worse than continuing with a low one (PyLaia −0.05% to +1.56% relative change); self-training on non-frequent writers 0.71% (PyLaia, not significant) to 2.88% — [Scius-Bertrand et al., ICDAR 2023](https://arodes.hes-so.ch/record/13180/files/Scius-Bertrand_2023_Bullinger_dataset.pdf).
- CTC fine-tuning study (19 manuscripts, mostly modern pretraining): writer-independent fine-tuning cut CER by 20–45% relative for 16–256 lines (abstract: 25% at 16 lines, 50% at 256); for writers already in the training set fine-tuning gave 6% (no augmentation) to 15% (with augmentation); "the more well-trained the baseline model, the greater and more stable" the gain — [Kohút & Hradiš](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.06308).
- German medieval mixed-model study: pretrained model out-of-box 6.22% CER; 2/4/8/16/32 pages gave 3.27/2.58/2.17/1.94/1.65%; from-scratch CER was 21.12/10.74/5.82/3.78/2.71%, so pretraining's relative advantage fell from 85% (2 pages) to 39% (32 pages). For a hand already in the pretraining data (Wernher-Wien) CER went 2.99 (0 pages) → 2.39 → 2.11 → 1.99 → 1.84 → 1.57 (32 pages) — [Reul et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.07661).
- Language-specific fine-tuning of the generic FoNDUE-GD (accuracy, %): PHILIUMM Latin test pages 90.78 → 94.86; Sardi 88.27 → 92.86; HTRomance French 17th–18th c. 86.71 → 89.14; Bastille (FoNDUE-FR-MSS-18) 86.58 → 90.74; AAEB French 16th–17th c. 82.98 → 88.00; Meyer von Schauensee (German 18th c.) 87.45 → 88.31; the authors note the least-represented languages gain most — [Gabay et al., Table 2](https://hal.science/hal-05063299/document).

**Better or different base**
- Montreal 17th-c. French (Transkribus; 25+ hands, ~1,500 transcribed pages): same model without base 25.40% CER, with the "Bailliage of Montréal" base 8.40% (−66%); another hand 18.60% → 11.34%. A bigger base from the same period (304,325 words, 5.00% validation CER) did worse for one hand than a smaller, lexically closer one (7.87% vs 5.88%, "an increase in errors of over 2%"); conclusion: base similarity, especially lexical, matters more than size. Single-hand models with 3.8k–36.5k training words had validation CER of 8.3–17.1% (best: Saint-Onge 13,149 words 8.30%, Adhémar 22,639 words 8.40%, Pruneau 8,846 words 9.00%; Maugue 36,510 words 11.34%); combining transcription protocol, language models and base choice raised one model's performance by more than 20% to a CER below 5% (the paper's Table 4 and its text are not fully consistent on the base-model numbers) — [Couture et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11146).
- Kiessling 2026: fine-tuning each PP-OCRv6 variant from the heterogeneous pretrained model on only 25% of CATMuS beat its own from-scratch counterpart trained on 100% "across all seven test sets"; more fine-tuning data "generally" lowers error, but for Czech performance fell as more CATMuS data were added (read as forgetting of cross-family knowledge); few-shot on four manuscripts: the kraken CRNN improved already with 20 lines, PP-OCRv6 improved only after 40 lines (one manuscript), 80 lines (two) or not within 80 lines (one), yet had the lower absolute CER from 40 lines on all four; combined standard deviation mostly <0.8 points — [arXiv 2609.20064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20064).
- TrOCR on a single Latin hand (Gwalther, 3,603 training lines): HTR+ 2.74% CER, TrOCR-large 2.55% (validation); on 13,843 lines of ~60 Bullinger writers HTR+ 21.13% vs TrOCR-large 16.53% (test) — [Ströbel et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.11008).
- Out-of-domain fine-tuning of the generic FoNDUE-GD with 13 pages of 18th-c. Italian letters: accuracy 80.68 → 86.06 (88.51 when starting from the French variant); French 20th-c. medical records 68.35 → 75.93 (77.37) — [Gabay et al.](https://hal.science/hal-05063299/document).

**Over-generalisation, domain gap and evaluation**
- Adding more diachronic CATMuS Medieval data degraded results on the best manuscripts ("likely due to over-generalization"); contextual metadata tokens (century, script, language) helped — [Kiessling & Clérice, CHR 2024](https://zenodo.org/records/14870764).
- Across 336 out-of-distribution cases, textual divergence is the most significant factor for generalisation, followed by visual divergence — [Garrido-Muñoz & Calvo-Zaragoza, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Garrido-Munoz_On_the_Generalization_of_Handwritten_Text_Recognition_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf).
- CATMuS Medieval: Kraken CER 4.7% on the random split vs 13.1% on the out-of-domain "Feature" split — [CATMuS paper](https://inria.hal.science/hal-04453952). TRIDIS: TrOCR 9.1% (random) vs 11.3% (outlier test); MiniCPM 10.2 vs 12.6 — [TRIDIS paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.22714) (the paper labels these "placeholder" results).

**Noisy aligned labels**
- Bullinger (109,627 training lines, auto-aligned): PyLaia 9.13% CER on the original test set, but on lines flagged for hyphenation issues 19.47% with original labels vs 12.89% with corrected labels; removing flagged lines from training (to 73–82% of the data) did not help (PyLaia 9.41–9.71%) — [Jungo et al., SOICT 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09037). On the authors' 100 manually corrected pages baseline PyLaia has CER 7.6% (subset 1) and 18.6% (subset 2); fine-tuning on CTC-aligned, confidence-filtered lines lowered this by 1.1 and 0.7 points (to 6.5% and 17.9%); model size also matters (1.3M → 6.4M parameters: CER 23.3% → 7.6% on subset 1) — [Peer et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07904).

**Rules of thumb (secondary, not opened)**: search summaries cite Transkribus guidance of at least 25 pages, a UCL study of 75–100 pages for a usable model, and a Greifswald project's observation that doubling the ground truth roughly halved CER (50,000–100,000 words for good models) — [Transkribus blog](https://blog.transkribus.org/en/how-to-improve-the-cer-of-your-model), [UCL Sloane study](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10072160/1/HTR_Sloane_MH_JN.pdf), [Greifswald](https://rechtsprechung-im-ostseeraum.archiv.uni-greifswald.de/2019/10/). These pages returned errors when opened here.

### Inferences
- Per-doubling arithmetic on the cited numbers (*Estimate*): Reul et al. pretrained curve 3.27 → 1.65 over four doublings is about −16% CER per doubling on average (range −11% to −21%); the in-training-hand curve (2.39 → 1.57 over four doublings) about −10% per doubling. Kohút's writer-in-training gain (6–15%) and Bullinger's (4–10%) are one-off, not per doubling.
- *Estimate with weak support.* Adding 297k aligned lines to the 63k existing lines (about 360k, 5.7× the data) is about 2.5 doublings. Applying 10–16% CER reduction per doubling (the range above) gives a multiplier of 0.77–0.65, i.e. roughly 7.95% → 5.1–6.1%, and only if the aligned lines were worth as much as clean ones. If a noisy line were worth half a clean line (an assumption, no source), the gain is about 1.3 doublings, i.e. roughly 6.4–7.0%. The per-doubling rates come from German medieval pages and few-page regimes and probably overstate returns at 60k+ lines. The only evidence on noisy lines at scale (Bullinger) shows that label noise inflates apparent error and that discarding flagged lines does not help.
- The first noisy-data step in the project (−4.2 points from 43k noisy lines) suggests aligned lines are valuable per line, but the second step (clean data, −2.5 points) shows clean lines still matter after noisy ones; the order (noisy first, clean last at lower learning rate) is the one used by PHILIUMM.
- Out-of-domain Latin/French GT is unlikely to help much by direct addition: the French and Dutch material is already in the FoNDUE-GD base, and the evidence that text/lexicon similarity dominates (Couture; Garrido-Muñoz) points to Leibniz-specific text, not generic Latin/French, as the useful extra data. Gains from generic data come mainly through a pretrained base (PP-OCRv6/Party), which already ingested most of it.
- A better base is the lever with the strongest published support (consistent −24% to −33% relative at matched fine-tuning), but evidence is for new manuscripts; for a writer with 60k+ lines the expected gain is smaller (the CRNN from scratch was competitive at 142k lines).
- Evaluation advice: the 7.95% is a line-level, same-distribution split; document-disjoint splits typically cost far more (CATMuS 4.7 → 13.1), so any comparison between bases or datasets should use a document-disjoint test set.

### Gaps
- No study reports CER vs training-set size for a single early-modern Latin/French writer beyond about 25k words; no paper found on adding hundreds of thousands of noisy aligned lines to a single-writer model.
- The Kiessling few-shot and CATMuS-tier curves are only in figures; exact values were not extractable.
- Hodel et al. 2021 (general vs specific Kurrent models) and the Ströbel et al. 2023 TrOCR adaptability paper were not opened.
- The three "rules of thumb" sources could not be opened.

### Inferences
- Because the current starting point (FoNDUE-GD_v2_ft_Leibniz) is CC BY-NC 4.0, further fine-tuning from it plausibly yields NC-bound weights; starting from FoNDUE-GD (CC BY 4.0), PP-OCRv6 or Party (Apache-2.0) avoids that on paper. This is an inference, not legal advice; the ShareAlike/NonCommercial status of weights trained on such data is not settled by any source I found.
- The Leibniz Legible reports describe the aligned lines as open-licence ground truth built from copyright-expired edition text and GWLB images under Public Domain Mark ([Zenodo 22782819](https://zenodo.org/records/22782819)); mixing in Bullinger (SA) or BEOL/Newton (NC) data would plausibly import those terms into the mix (my inference, not legal advice).

### Gaps
- No source found that states how ShareAlike or NonCommercial datasets propagate to trained model weights.
- Per-resource licences inside BEOL, the images' terms for Science in the Making, and the actual licences of the unreachable FoNDUE repositories were not verified.
